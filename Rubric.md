# Recruiter-Facing Resume Evaluation Rubric
**Evidence-Based Screening & Pedagogical Assessment Guide**

> **Scoring Scale:**  
> **1** = Weak | **2** = Partial | **3** = Acceptable | **4** = Strong | **5** = Excellent  
> **Total Possible Score:** 35 Points

---

## 1. Role Alignment

| Score | Performance Descriptor |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1 – Weak** | Generic; weak match to the target role; reviewer must infer relevance. |
| **2 – Partial** | Some relevant experience, but not clearly tied to the role. |
| **3 – Acceptable** | Relevant experience is present, but alignment is uneven. |
| **4 – Strong** | Strong match to the role’s core requirements. |
| **5 – Excellent** | Very clear fit; strongest signals are obvious, prioritized, and immediate. |

### Relevant Literature & Grounding
* **Spence, M. (1973).** *Job Market Signaling.* Quarterly Journal of Economics, 87(3), 355–374.  
  * **Relevance:** Résumés function as signals of candidate productivity and fit under conditions of asymmetric information and recruiter uncertainty.
* **Rivera, L. A. (2012).** *Hiring as Cultural Matching: The Case of Elite Professional Service Firms.* American Sociological Review, 77(6), 999–1022.  
  * **Relevance:** Perceived fit includes contextual and role matching, not just isolated technical credentials.
* **Wingate, T., Robie, C., Powell, D., & Bourdage, J. (2024/2025).** *Signal-Based Résumé Research and Hiring Assessment Work.*  
  * **Relevance:** Direct, observable evidence of alignment yields substantially higher screening utility than generic phrasing.

## 2. Evidence Quality / Receipts

| Score | Performance Descriptor |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1 – Weak** | Vague claims; little proof of actual work performed. |
| **2 – Partial** | Some concrete details, but limited ownership or verification. |
| **3 – Acceptable** | Adequate proof, though still somewhat generic. |
| **4 – Strong** | Most claims are supported by specific facts, tools, and responsibilities. |
| **5 – Excellent** | Claims are specific, traceable, defensible, and interview-ready. |

### Relevant Literature & Grounding
* **Wingate, T., et al. (2024/2025).** *Signal-Based Résumé Research and Hiring Assessment Work.*  
  * **Relevance:** The communicative validity of a résumé increases significantly when claims are concrete, specific, and verifiable.
* **Risavy, S. D., et al. (2022).** *Resumes vs. application forms: Why the stubborn reliance on resumes?* Frontiers in Psychology.  
  * **Relevance:** Résumés are imperfect signaling instruments; providing verifiable evidence mitigates evaluator variance and improves screening quality.
* **Spence, M. (1973).** *Job Market Signaling.*  
  * **Relevance:** Signals provide zero screening utility unless they are perceived as credible, interpretable, and costly to fake.

## 3. Impact and Outcomes

| Score | Performance Descriptor |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1 – Weak** | Lists duties/tasks only; no sense of result or deliverables. |
| **2 – Partial** | Some outcomes mentioned, but weak, uncontextualized, or unclear. |
| **3 – Acceptable** | Outcomes are present but not consistently quantified or explained. |
| **4 – Strong** | Clear impact and business/user relevance documented. |
| **5 – Excellent** | Strong evidence of measurable outcome, scope, or meaningful architectural/business change. |

### Relevant Literature & Grounding
* **Spence, M. (1973).** *Job Market Signaling.*  
  * **Relevance:** Evaluators interpret concrete performance indicators and verifiable deliverables as far stronger ability signals than nominal job titles.
* **Risavy, S. D., et al. (2022).** *Resumes vs. application forms: Why the stubborn reliance on resumes?*  
  * **Relevance:** Documented business outcomes and scope provide recruiters with higher diagnostic predictive value than task lists.

---

## 4. Interview Defensibility

| Score | Performance Descriptor |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1 – Weak** | Hard to discuss; weak story structure; vulnerable to probing. |
| **2 – Partial** | Can explain some points, but lacks architectural or contextual depth. |
| **3 – Acceptable** | Reasonably defendable, but shallow in edge cases or technical trade-offs. |
| **4 – Strong** | Easy to expand in an interview; naturally invites technical follow-up questions. |
| **5 – Excellent** | Clear story arc; candidate can speak with mastery on decisions, trade-offs, and impact. |

### Relevant Literature & Grounding
* **Rivera, L. A. (2012).** *Hiring as Cultural Matching.*  
  * **Relevance:** Résumé screening serves as an entry ticket to verbal evaluation; points on the page must hold up during direct conversational interrogation.
* **Wingate, T., et al. (2024/2025).** *Signal-Based Résumé Research and Hiring Assessment Work.*  
  * **Relevance:** Written claims that can withstand rigorous situational and technical follow-up checks are dramatically more predictive than polished, shallow assertions.

## 5. Clarity and Readability

| Score | Performance Descriptor |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1 – Weak** | Hard to scan; dense, cluttered, or structurally confusing. |
| **2 – Partial** | Understandable, but requires high cognitive effort to parse. |
| **3 – Acceptable** | Mostly readable, but visual hierarchy could be sharper. |
| **4 – Strong** | Clear structure and layout; very easy for a recruiter to skim in under 30 seconds. |
| **5 – Excellent** | Exceptional visual hierarchy; strongest qualifications and signals stand out immediately. |

### Relevant Literature & Grounding
* **Reber, R., Schwarz, N., & Winkielman, P. (2004).** *Processing Fluency and Aesthetic Pleasure: Is Beauty in the Perceiver's Processing Experience?* Personality and Social Psychology Review, 8(4), 364–382.  
  * **Relevance:** Cognitive ease directly impacts document evaluation; higher processing fluency causes evaluators to rate candidate information as more credible and persuasive.
* **Sterkens, P., et al. (2023).** *Costly mistakes: Why and when spelling errors in resumes jeopardise interview chances.* PLOS ONE, 18(4), e0283280.  
  * **Relevance:** Structural layout friction and visual disorganization trigger negative inferences regarding candidate conscientiousness and organizational ability.

## 6. Authenticity / Responsible AI Use

| Score | Performance Descriptor |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1 – Weak** | Fabricated or inflated claims; generic, ungrounded AI buzzwords. |
| **2 – Partial** | Heavy reliance on generic boilerplate or weakly verified phrasing. |
| **3 – Acceptable** | Mostly honest and accurate, but contains vague generalities. |
| **4 – Strong** | Human-reviewed, realistic, credible, and grounded in candidate experience. |
| **5 – Excellent** | Meticulously honest, concrete, defensible, and intentionally refined. |

### Relevant Literature & Grounding
* **Bertrand, M., & Mullainathan, S. (2004).** *Are Emily and Greg More Employable Than Lakisha and Jamal?* American Economic Review, 94(4), 991–1013.  
  * **Relevance:** Evaluators scrutinize signaling markers under bias and uncertainty; factual verification and grounded credibility prevent negative discounting.
* **Wingate, T., et al. (2024/2025).** *Signal-Based Résumé Research and Hiring Assessment Work.*  
  * **Relevance:** Synthetic, hallucinated, or ungrounded generative AI language degrades signal clarity and raises red flags during technical cross-examination.
* **AI Governance & Algorithmic Decision-Making Literature.**  
  * **Relevance:** Rigorous human-in-the-loop validation is essential to prevent synthetic hallucination, algorithmic overclaiming, and inflated credentials.

## 7. Professional Accuracy

| Score | Performance Descriptor |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1 – Weak** | Frequent spelling, grammar, date, or formatting inconsistencies. |
| **2 – Partial** | Noticeable errors, though not completely disqualifying. |
| **3 – Acceptable** | Generally accurate with minor superficial cleanup needed. |
| **4 – Strong** | Thoroughly accurate, professional, and mechanically polished. |
| **5 – Excellent** | Flawless internal coherence, precise timeline dates, and uniform typography. |

### Relevant Literature & Grounding
* **Sterkens, P., et al. (2023).** *Costly mistakes: Why and when spelling errors in resumes jeopardise interview chances.* PLOS ONE, 18(4), e0283280.  
  * **Relevance:** Typos, timeline gaps, and grammatical mistakes function as potent negative signals that substantially reduce screening call-back rates.
* **Reber, R., Schwarz, N., & Winkielman, P. (2004).** *Processing Fluency and Aesthetic Pleasure.*  
  * **Relevance:** Textual correctness avoids cognitive interruptions, keeping the recruiter focused on candidate qualifications rather than mechanical defects.

## Score Interpretation & Action Plan

| Composite Score | Category | Recruiter Diagnosis & Actionable Next Steps |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **30 – 35** | **Strong / Hiring-Ready** | **Immediate Submission:** High-signal document. Evidence is defensible, verified, and tailored. Ready for active role matching. |
| **24 – 29** | **Good / Targeted Refinement** | **Minor Edits Needed:** Solid candidate background obscured by slight ambiguity. Tighten bullet points with stronger metrics and remove minor visual clutter. |
| **17 – 23** | **Weak / Generic** | **Substantive Rework Required:** Overly task-centric or ungrounded. Candidate must extract concrete deliverables ("receipts") and align terminology with target job requirements. |
| **≤ 16** | **Not Yet Competitive** | **Full Overhaul Required:** High recruiter rejection risk. Rebuild resume structure from scratch using confirmed facts, verified timelines, and proof-of-work projects. |
