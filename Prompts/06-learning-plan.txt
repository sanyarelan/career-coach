Create a learning plan for the confirmed gaps in my selected target role.

Use my existing skills, weekly available hours, budget, and application
timeline. If those are unknown, ask before scheduling.

Prioritize gaps by:
- Importance to the target role.
- Prerequisites.
- Feasibility within my available time.
- Value across other roles I am interested in.

For each priority gap, specify:
- The concrete capability I need to demonstrate.
- A short baseline exercise to check my starting level.
- Learning activities.
- Estimated effort as a range, with assumptions.
- A practice task or project.
- Observable completion criteria.
- Evidence I can discuss in an interview or eventually add to my résumé.

Build a weekly schedule that fits my actual time budget, including practice,
review, and catch-up time.

Prefer a small number of relevant resources. When browsing is available,
verify links, current availability, and disclosed costs; prioritize official
documentation and credible educational sources. Include a free alternative
where feasible. Otherwise label resource suggestions as unverified.

Distinguish:
- What I can reasonably learn before applying.
- What I can learn while applying.
- What requires longer-term experience or formal qualifications.

Include:
1. A prioritized gap list.
2. A realistic weekly plan.
3. One integrated project where appropriate.
4. A checkpoint to adjust the plan after the first week.
5. A clear readiness test.

Do not guarantee mastery, certification, employment, or that course completion
alone demonstrates job competence.
